This phase is almost entirely Storyteller work. Player input is limited to ranking concrete campaign seeds. [Character creation](/character-creation/) comes later.

If you are a player reading ahead: you only need the constraints and the seeds you are asked to rank. Everything else on this page is for the person preparing the table.

## Interest Signal

Players receive only a ranked list of campaign seeds and the system constraints. Nothing else written. If someone asks about a specific name, answer in two spoken sentences at most.

<p class="kod-lane__title">Constraints (state once)</p>
<p><strong>No magic.</strong> Pre-industrial tools and knowledge only. Ordinary human beings.<br /><strong>The community is the real protagonist.</strong> If it is destroyed, the campaign ends.<br /><strong>Death is permanent.</strong></p>
</div>
<div class="kod-lane kod-lane--player">
<p class="kod-lane__title">What players do</p>

The seeds below are **examples only**. Replace them freely. Players rank their top three.

A good seed uses a **real or invented place name** (not a vague label like “river country”), names a **community**, a **real pressure** (war, law, plague, tax, famine, migration), and a **decision that cannot wait**. It is not a list of horrors. Grim reality is the weather of the age — not the product you are selling.

## Worldbuilding Practices

Do these **in order**. Prep **situations** — unstable circumstances, people with goals, places under pressure — not a fixed sequence of scenes. The story is what happens when the table acts. You may know more than you show; you do not open with a lore dump.

The nine steps below are the method. Read them as prose first — you must be able to reread a step without opening the stepper. The stepper is a **worked walkthrough** of **The Vardmark at Kelarn’s Bend**. A second worked world, **Iceland after the burnings**, sits in the prose so the method has to transfer.

### 1. Spectrum

Lock tightly historical, semi-historical, or fully invented — and keep it.

**How.** Write the spectrum on the first line of your notes. Historical: real year, real season, real law, real armies constrain you. You may invent household names; you may not invent a harvest that the climate would not give, or a levy no lord of that year could raise. Semi-historical: change **one** hinge (the settlement held; the theme never returned), then honour every consequence of that change. Invented: hunger, weather, and power have the same gravity as history. Made-up names are fine. Inventing away the cost of living is not.

**Fake work.** Spectrum as flavour (“Viking-ish”). A historical year used as costume. An invented world where the conquerors already know how to farm the land they just burned.

**Two worlds.** Vardmark: fully invented, occupation after a sack. Iceland: tightly historical — hay, driftwood rights, blood-price, Althing in weeks. Both have a decision that cannot wait.

### 2. How they live

How does this people stay alive from season to season? Work, food, shelter, who settles quarrels. Where characters “should” be when they are not on a job.

**How.** Name the hard season and who is free in it. If you cannot say how they get through late winter, stop. Do not give conquerors the skills of the people they destroyed unless play earns that change. When player plans pull people off the watch or the forage, the community should notice.

**Fake work.** Three adjectives (“hardy, proud, warlike”). A people with no work and no hungry month.

**Two worlds.** Vardmark: war-following, wagons, looted grain, no plough. Iceland: hay first, then court; the living still have to bring the winter in.

### 3. What must not fail

Name the concrete things whose failure ends this community as a community. Stores, rights, places, tools, bonds — countable and at risk.

**How.** Five to eight items. Each should be able to drive a session if threatened, stolen, bargained, or defended. Prefer things that force choices between households.

**Fake work.** Lore nouns (“honour”, “the old ways”) with nothing you can steal or burn.

**Two worlds.** Vardmark: remaining loot, the ford, wagons, captives who know the fields, no living king’s seal. Iceland: hay, driftwood rights, unpaid blood-price, the following that will walk to a stronger goði if court is lost.

### 4. Strain now

One present-tense strain sentence per item. What is wrong this season. Who is pressing. What cannot wait.

**How.** Deadlines, faces, pressure. If nobody acts before the next moon, the community loses options. Prep what happens if the table does nothing. If the sentence could sit on any map, it is not local enough.

**Fake work.** Mood without a clock. “Tensions are high.”

### 5. Who holds it

Name only the people you need to make those strains playable. A few individuals and houses with wants and fears.

**How.** For each: what they want this season, what they will not forgive, what they do if left alone. Leverage, not titles.

**Fake work.** A cast list. Ranks with no want.

### 6. Leave blanks

Do not pre-solve the social map. You may hold private notes. You do not open with a lecture on who sits where.

**How.** They learn standing when an order is obeyed — or is not.

**Fake work.** An unfinished notes file called “mystery.” Blanks you have not actually thought about.

### 7. What they can see

Three or four visible marks of recent history — things anyone can point at without a speech.

**How.** When players ask what happened here, answer with what they can walk to and touch. Repeat marks until the table changes them.

**Fake work.** Wallpaper tragedy in every room.

### 8. Themes and threads

Craft themes and side threads — then leave engagement to the players. Weave them through the main pressure. Do not force a path.

**How.** Each thread is a situation: people with goals, pressure that worsens if ignored, more than one way in.

**Fake work.** A sermon. A required subplot.

### 9. Fortunes

Set starting [Fortunes](/echoes/#fortunes) as soft 0–3 **from the facts above**. Do not set them to sound epic.

**How — derivation.** Surplus from stores and the hard season (Vardmark loot is thin → 1; Iceland hay is the session’s clock → 1 or 2). Cohesion from who already pulls apart (Torven vs House Asren → 1; two followings after burnings → 1). Vitality from how many hands remain. Standing from how outsiders treat *anyone* of this people. Tradition from whether they still know who they are — not who they wish they were. If the board looks comfortable after conquest and winter, re-check the strain list.

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="1" data-step-title="Spectrum">
<p><strong>Lock the spectrum</strong> — tightly historical, semi-historical, or fully invented — and keep it for the whole campaign.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Fully invented: the Vardmark occupy Kelarn’s Bend after they helped sack Kelarn — conquerors on ground they do not know how to farm. Made-up names are fine; hunger, weather, and armed neighbours still work as in history.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Write the spectrum on the first line of your notes. Historical: real seasons, law, and armies constrain you — use that. Semi-historical: change one hinge, then honour the consequences. Invented: same gravity of hunger, weather, and power. Do not invent away the cost of living, and do not invent cruelty for spectacle.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="2" data-step-title="How they live" hidden>
<p><strong>How does this people stay alive from season to season?</strong> Work, food, shelter, who settles quarrels. Where characters “should” be when they are not on a job.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Most of the Vardmark still live as a war-following: wagons, hide tents, and half-raised halls at Kelarn’s Bend. They eat looted grain, drive stolen cattle, hunt the reed-edge, and send out forage parties. A few smiths keep gear fit for the next fight. Children mind stock from the march. They do not plough; the people who knew these fields are dead, fled, or held as captives. There is little leisure — only the next take, the next watch, the next argument over who owns what was stolen.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Material life is the foundation of the world. Know the hard season and who is free in it. If play never touches how people eat and work, the place feels hollow. When player plans pull people off the watch or the forage, the community should notice — that friction is story. If you cannot say how they get through late winter, fix that before you invent another faction. Do not give conquerors the skills of the people they just destroyed unless play earns that change.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="3" data-step-title="What must not fail" hidden>
<p><strong>Name the concrete things whose failure ends this community as a community.</strong> Stores, rights, places, tools, bonds — countable and at risk.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>The remaining looted grain and cattle at the Bend; the ford at Kelarn’s Bend; the wagons and mounts that still let them move as a war-band; control of the captives who know the fields and fords; the claim on the burned capital’s remaining stores; the absence of any living king of Kelarn who could lawfully regrant or redeem the land.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Five to eight items. Each should be able to drive a session if threatened, stolen, bargained, or defended. Prefer things that force choices between households (who eats the last sack of grain? who holds the ford at night? who owns a captive who can work the soil?). An empty throne can be one of these — not as backstory to recite, but as a gap everyone feels when a decision needs a name on it.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="4" data-step-title="Strain now" hidden>
<p><strong>One present-tense strain sentence per item.</strong> What is wrong this season. Who is pressing. What cannot wait.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Looted grain will not feed the following through late winter. The Red Banner has camped a day’s ride upriver and is stripping the same countryside. Marsh survivors will only trade fish if the Vardmark return captives from the sack. The ford is still contested after dark. Without a king of Kelarn, no seal can make the occupation “lawful” — every bargain is personal and may be broken by the next stronger hand.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Strain is a situation the players can walk into, not a monologue. Prefer deadlines, faces, and pressure over adjectives. If nobody acts before the next moon, the community loses options — food, ground, or unity. Prep what would happen if the table did nothing. If the sentence could sit on any map, make it local to <em>this</em> place and season — use real or invented names, not vague labels.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="5" data-step-title="Who holds it" hidden>
<p><strong>Name only the people you need to make those strains playable.</strong> A few individuals and houses with wants and fears — enough to bind scenes together, not a cast list for a novel.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>War-leader Torven, who means to keep the following as a pure war-band and will not stay still. House Asren, which holds most of the remaining loot and the best captives, and wants them worked like property, not freed or traded cheap. Marsh elder Sera of the Kelarn remnant, willing to deal only if captives come home. Rival captain Jorun of the Red Banner, who wants the ford for himself. The absent king of Kelarn is only a fact: no seal, no court, every promise weaker for it.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Prep <em>people with leverage and goals</em>, not ranks or titles to recite. For each: what they want this season, what they will not forgive, what they do if left alone. Name places and people when it ties the story together — not to dump a gazetteer in session one. Leave room for the table to meet them in play and form their own judgments.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="6" data-step-title="Leave blanks" hidden>
<p><strong>Do not pre-solve the social map for the players.</strong> Who is trusted, who leads, who is owed — that is best discovered in play through action, rumour, and consequence. You may hold private notes. You do not open with a lecture on who sits where.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>You know Torven expects obedience and House Asren expects a voice over loot and captives. You do not tell the table the order of standing. They learn it when someone gives an order and is obeyed — or is not — and when a bargain holds or breaks.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Connections, standing, and hierarchy emerge from fiction. Naming every rung of power up front is a lore dump: it kills immersion and steals discovery. Hold what you need for consistency; reveal through scenes. If a bookkeeping diagram of standing exists for your own use, keep it off the table until play has earned the right to fill it. The point of this step is blank space the players walk into.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="7" data-step-title="What they can see" hidden>
<p><strong>Three or four visible marks of recent history</strong> — things anyone can point at without a speech.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Blackened orchard rows on the approach to ruined Kelarn. Wagon-ruts and picket lines where the Vardmark war-camp has sat all winter. The ford at Kelarn’s Bend marked with poles and trophies from both banks. Smoke from the Red Banner camp upriver on clear mornings.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Show, do not lecture. When players ask “what happened here?”, answer with what they can walk to and touch. Repeat marks across sessions until the table has changed them. Conquest leaves traces; you need not decorate every scene with them.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="8" data-step-title="Themes and threads" hidden>
<p><strong>Craft themes and side threads — then leave engagement to the players.</strong> Weave them through the main pressure so the world feels deep. Do not force a path through them.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Threads you hold ready: Sera’s remnant wants captives returned; the Red Banner wants the ford; Torven wants another campaign before the loot runs out; House Asren wants the captives broken to field work; a clerk still claims to speak for the dead king of Kelarn’s seal. Themes under them: occupation without knowing how to rule, guilt or denial for the sack, legitimacy without a throne, the cost of winning. None of these is a required path. Any can rise if the players lean that way — and each advances if they do not.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Side stories are not clutter; they are how a campaign feels lived-in. Prep each thread as a <em>situation</em>: people with goals, pressure that worsens if ignored, more than one way in. Weave themes through the main strain — food, ground, legitimacy, the cost of conquest — without scripting which scenes the table must play. Participation rests with the players. That is not “no side plots”; it is no railroading. If they never take a thread, the world still moves; if they take it, you already know who wants what.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="9" data-step-title="Fortunes" hidden>
<p><strong>Set starting <a href="/echoes/#fortunes">Fortunes</a></strong> (Vitality, Cohesion, Surplus, Standing, Tradition) as soft 0–3 from the facts above. Store them when the campaign record is created. Early play may correct them.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Vitality 2 (war-hardened, but worn). Cohesion 1 (Torven’s pure war-band and House Asren’s captive-drivers already pull apart). Surplus 1 (loot thin, no real harvest of their own). Standing 1 (neighbours treat the Vardmark as raiders and despoilers, not a realm). Tradition 2 (they still know who they were on the march — not yet what occupation makes of them).</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Fortunes are community weather for every scene — PCs and NPCs. Derive them from material facts, not from a wish to sound epic. If the board looks comfortable after conquest and winter, re-check the strain list.</div>
</div>
