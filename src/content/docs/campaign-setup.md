This phase is almost entirely Storyteller work. Player input is limited to ranking concrete campaign seeds. [Character creation](/character-creation/) comes later.

If you are a player reading ahead: you only need the constraints and the seeds you are asked to rank. Everything else on this page is for the person preparing the table.

----------

## Interest Signal

Players receive only a ranked list of campaign seeds and the system constraints. Nothing else written. If someone asks about a specific name, answer in two spoken sentences at most.

<div class="kod-lanes not-content">
<div class="kod-lane kod-lane--st">
<p class="kod-lane__title">Constraints (state once)</p>
<p><strong>No magic.</strong> Pre-industrial tools and knowledge only. Ordinary human beings.<br /><strong>The community is the real protagonist.</strong> If it is destroyed, the campaign ends.<br /><strong>Death is permanent.</strong></p>
</div>
<div class="kod-lane kod-lane--player">
<p class="kod-lane__title">What players do</p>
<p>Rank your top three seeds. Ask at most two spoken sentences about any name. Wait for the Storyteller to frame the community — then create characters.</p>
</div>
</div>

The seeds below are **examples only**. Replace them freely. Players rank their top three.

A good seed uses a **settlement-scale name** — a peel, a township, a harbour, a city a person could walk to — not a country or a vague label like “river country”. It names a **community**, a **real pressure** (war, law, plague, tax, famine, migration) with a deadline that can recur across seasons, and a **collision of two legitimate claims**. It is not a tactic menu. It is not a single lawsuit. It is not a list of horrors. Do not list the community’s destruction as a choice on the card. Grim reality is the weather of the age — not the product you are selling.

<div class="kod-seed kod-seed--hist">
<p class="kod-seed__title">Mangerton, Liddesdale, 1323</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Who</strong> — A peel-township in the Liddel dale: cattle, moss, a following that has answered to whichever warden last rode through. Douglas men, English riders, and the surnames share the same grass.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — This March the Earl of Carlisle, Andrew Harclay, is hanged for a private peace with Bruce. The West March has no lawful protector. Both kings’ men will be in the dale before the hay.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The custom of living as a dale-people against a harvest that now exists only under a named lord — and every lord this year is a traitor to someone.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — The next armed household writes Mangerton onto its roll. The other side treats the cattle as prey.</li>
</ul>

<div class="kod-seed kod-seed--hist">
<p class="kod-seed__title">Kabar at the Aranyos salt, 899</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Who</strong> — A Kabar war-following wintering above the Roman salt-shafts of Torda: horses, wagons, a Khazar-derived oath. In the valley, Slavic and Avar-remnant salt-people with a župan, pits, and a plough. Árpád’s host is weeks of grass away.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — The seven have sent for the salt as tribute. The Kabar chieftain who rode van claims it as the price of alliance. The shafts flood if the salt-people flee or are butchered.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The Kabar right to remain a people apart — van, old oath, horse — against the salt that only feeds a people who sit. Sitting is how the seven will swallow them.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — Tribute goes to the seven, or the shafts are stripped once. Next spring the Kabar are a name on Árpád’s count, or a following without salt.</li>

<div class="kod-seed kod-seed--semi">
<p class="kod-seed__title">Kaisereia, 1072</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Break</strong> — The 1067 sack left the city sparsely inhabited for decades. Here the civic body still holds the walls, one year after Manzikert, while the empire eats itself in the capital.</li>
<li><strong>Who</strong> — The remnant in the walls: Greek households, Armenian houses, the Basiliad’s sick and poor, a doux who may already have one horse pointed west. The shrine of Basil still stands. The roads are dead.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — Forage parties at the gates. Grain inside is counted in weeks. An envoy will offer terms that unmake the name. A Byzantine officer in the citadel has a price.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The right to remain Kaisereia — gates, law, the shrine — against the right of the people inside to live this winter. Tribute or an opened gate may feed children and end the city.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — The citadel is sold or starved. Kaisereia becomes a name for a camp at the foot of Argaeus.</li>
</ul>

<div class="kod-seed kod-seed--semi">
<p class="kod-seed__title">Straumfjǫrðr, five winters held</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Break</strong> — Historically the landing was a short camp. Here they have held five winters.</li>
<li><strong>Who</strong> — A landnám that did not go home: turf halls at the fjord of currents, a forge, women and a child. Greenlanders who named this for plenty.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — Hunt and fish have failed before. Illness in the turf. Iron almost gone. The people of the strand use the same bird-cliffs and seal-ice.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The landnám’s hope-right — we crossed to live; the child is owed this ground — against the strand-people’s right to cliffs, seal, and timber the halls already take. Trade can become kinship. A killing cannot.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — A taking of the cliffs to feed the halls. Trade becomes blood. The landnám becomes a raid-camp, or it starves.</li>
</ul>

<p class="kod-seed__title">Aspalath, after the count was stabbed</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Who</strong> — A limestone harbour-commune: olive, cisterns, salt, a loading crane. House Velatri and House Orsani still sit the Council that elects the count. House Moro holds the salt and unloads from its own warehouse. The shepherds of Osoje bring cheese and are not burghers.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — Count Rade Velatri was stabbed on the cathedral steps at vintage. The election is this moon. A clerk holds a letter: Albaran had been paying the count. Vessara’s ships are a night’s sail off and want rowers. House Moro will put men in the marketplace if the Council elects Rade’s son, who is twelve, and shuts them out.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The old houses’ right to elect the count from their own blood against House Moro’s right to a say in a city they now feed. Both are Aspalath. Albaran and Vessara have a hand in both.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — The Council elects the boy. Moro’s men take the marketplace. Albaran and Vessara pick opposite sides. Cargo starts landing at Ravna across the bay.</li>

<p class="kod-seed__title">Lychnis, in the pestilence</p>
<ul class="kod-seed__facts">
<li><strong>Who</strong> — Net-houses on a lake that does not reach the sea. Old families still divide the catch by custom. Plague in the lanes. The island monastery is now lazaretto and last granary.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure</strong> — The pestilence does not slacken. In the square, Velesin offers a plan: seal the island, pull the boats, he holds the roll of the living. Three days’ march off, the Saryn host, who say they are the Scourge of the Gods.</li>
<li><strong>Collision</strong> — The custom of remaining a people that buries its own against Velesin’s claim that the living have the right to live by cutting the sick away. Giving him the roll unmakes the people in order to save a count of bodies.</li>
<li><strong>If left</strong> — Velesin takes the square. The island is sealed. The Saryn arrive to a people that has already judged itself.</li>

## Worldbuilding Practices

Do these **in order**. Prep **situations** — unstable circumstances, people with goals, places under pressure — not a fixed sequence of scenes. The story is what happens when the table acts. You may know more than you show; you do not open with a lore dump.

The worked thread below follows **Aspalath, after the count was stabbed**. Change the seed; keep the method.

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="1" data-step-title="Spectrum">
<p><strong>Lock the spectrum</strong> — tightly historical, semi-historical, or fully invented — and keep it for the whole campaign.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Fully invented: Aspalath is a limestone harbour-commune with an elected count, olive, salt, and a loading crane. Made-up names are fine; hunger, weather, and armed neighbours still work as in history. House Velatri, House Moro, Albaran, and Vessara are as real as any family that eats there.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Write the spectrum on the first line of your notes. Historical: real seasons, law, and armies constrain you — use that. Semi-historical: change one hinge, then honour the consequences. Invented: same gravity of hunger, weather, and power. Do not invent away the cost of living, and do not invent cruelty for spectacle.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="2" data-step-title="How they live" hidden>
<p><strong>How does this people stay alive from season to season?</strong> Work, food, shelter, who settles quarrels. Where characters “should” be when they are not on a job.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Most of Aspalath lives by the harbour and the terraces: olive press in season, salt pans in the heat, fish when the boats are not taken for a galley, cistern-water all year. House Velatri and House Orsani eat from old oil and the Council’s due on cargo. House Moro eats from salt and a warehouse that unloads without the commune’s crane. Shepherds from Osoje come down with cheese and sleep outside the walls. The cathedral doles grain at the door, in Latin; boat families bury in their own tongue. When people are not “on a job” they are at the press, the pans, the crane, or the marketplace — not at leisure.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Material life is the foundation of the world. Know the hard season and who is free in it. If play never touches how people eat and work, the place feels hollow. When player plans pull people off the watch or the forage, the community should notice — that friction is story. If you cannot say how they get through late winter, fix that before you invent another faction. Do not give conquerors the skills of the people they just destroyed unless play earns that change.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="3" data-step-title="What must not fail" hidden>
<p><strong>Name the concrete things whose failure ends this community as a community.</strong> Stores, rights, places, tools, bonds — countable and at risk.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>This year’s olive; the cisterns; the salt pans; the loading crane; the privilege that makes ships unload here; the election of the count; who is named for the next galley; the harbour chain; the clerk who can read the Albaran letter; the cheese-right of Osoje, who are not burghers.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Five to eight items. Each should be able to drive a session if threatened, stolen, bargained, or defended. Prefer things that force choices between households (who eats the last sack of grain? who holds the ford at night? who owns a captive who can work the soil?). An empty throne can be one of these — not as backstory to recite, but as a gap everyone feels when a decision needs a name on it.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="4" data-step-title="Strain now" hidden>
<p><strong>One present-tense strain sentence per item.</strong> What is wrong this season. Who is pressing. What cannot wait.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Count Rade Velatri was stabbed on the cathedral steps at vintage; the election is this moon. A clerk holds a letter: Albaran had been paying the count. Vessara’s ships are a night’s sail off and want rowers. The See of Loryn will name the bishop on an older privilege. House Moro will put men in the marketplace if the Council elects Rade’s son, who is twelve, and shuts them out. The olive is thin. Someone will die before the oil is pressed. If nobody acts, the Council elects the boy, Moro’s men take the marketplace, and cargo starts landing at Ravna across the bay.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Strain is a situation the players can walk into, not a monologue. Prefer deadlines, faces, and pressure over adjectives. If nobody acts before the next moon, the community loses options — food, ground, or unity. Prep what would happen if the table did nothing. If the sentence could sit on any map, make it local to <em>this</em> place and season — use real or invented names, not vague labels.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="5" data-step-title="Who holds it" hidden>
<p><strong>Name only the people you need to make those strains playable.</strong> A few individuals and houses with wants and fears — enough to bind scenes together, not a cast list for a novel.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Orsa Velatri, sister of the dead count, who wants the Council to elect his son, who is twelve. Piero Moro, who holds the salt and a warehouse that unloads without the commune’s crane, and will put men in the marketplace if his house gets no vote. Luca Bandi, consul for Vessara, in a rented house, with coin for rowers. Anselm, legate of Loryn, who will refuse a burial if the chapter is crossed. Vuk Osojanin, who brings cheese from the hills and is not a burgher. Agnese Orsani, who keeps the account of what the commune still owes the last galley’s widows. The clerk who holds the Albaran letter — a person with a sleeve, not a title.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Prep <em>people with leverage and goals</em>, not ranks or titles to recite. For each: what they want this season, what they will not forgive, what they do if left alone. Name places and people when it ties the story together — not to dump a gazetteer in session one. Leave room for the table to meet them in play and form their own judgments.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="6" data-step-title="Leave blanks" hidden>
<p><strong>Do not pre-solve the social map for the players.</strong> Who is trusted, who leads, who is owed — that is best discovered in play through action, rumour, and consequence. You may hold private notes. You do not open with a lecture on who sits where.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>You know Orsa Velatri expects the Council to elect the boy, and Piero Moro expects his house to sit the Council. You do not tell the table the order of standing. They learn it when someone names a count and is obeyed — or is not — and when a letter is read aloud or burned.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Connections, standing, and hierarchy emerge from fiction. Naming every rung of power up front is a lore dump: it kills immersion and steals discovery. Hold what you need for consistency; reveal through scenes. If a bookkeeping diagram of standing exists for your own use, keep it off the table until play has earned the right to fill it. The point of this step is blank space the players walk into.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="7" data-step-title="What they can see" hidden>
<p><strong>Three or four visible marks of recent history</strong> — things anyone can point at without a speech.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Blood scrubbed from the cathedral steps and still in the grain of the stone. Moro’s warehouse with no mark of the commune’s crane. Vessaran hulls at first light across the bay. Agnese’s account-book carried into the marketplace. Albaran seal-wax on a house that should not have it.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Show, do not lecture. When players ask “what happened here?”, answer with what they can walk to and touch. Repeat marks across sessions until the table has changed them. Conquest leaves traces; you need not decorate every scene with them.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="8" data-step-title="Themes and threads" hidden>
<p><strong>Craft themes and side threads — then leave engagement to the players.</strong> Weave them through the main pressure so the world feels deep. Do not force a path through them.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Threads you hold ready: Orsa wants the Council to elect the boy; Piero wants his house to sit the Council; Vessara wants rowers and the harbour; Loryn wants the bishop; Vuk will sell cheese to either side and leave if the hills are forgotten; Agnese will read the widows’ account in the marketplace; the clerk will live or die by whether the letter is opened. Themes under them: an election that still happens while the old houses hollow out; new wealth that actually feeds the city; foreign hands in both camps; who counts as a burgher. None of these is a required path. Any can rise if the players lean that way — and each advances if they do not.</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Side stories are not clutter; they are how a campaign feels lived-in. Prep each thread as a <em>situation</em>: people with goals, pressure that worsens if ignored, more than one way in. Weave themes through the main strain — food, ground, legitimacy, the cost of conquest — without scripting which scenes the table must play. Participation rests with the players. That is not “no side plots”; it is no railroading. If they never take a thread, the world still moves; if they take it, you already know who wants what.</div>
</div>

<div class="kod-widget__panel" data-panel-id="9" data-step-title="Fortunes" hidden>
<p><strong>Set starting <a href="/echoes/#fortunes">Fortunes</a></strong> (Vitality, Cohesion, Surplus, Standing, Tradition) as soft 0–3 from the facts above. Store them when the campaign record is created. Early play may correct them.</p>
<div class="kod-step-example"><strong>Example</strong>Vitality 1 (thin olive year; the count dead on the steps; the marketplace already tasting blood). Cohesion 1 (Velatri and Moro already pull apart; Osoje are not burghers). Surplus 2 (salt and cargo still move; the oil is thin). Standing 2 (ships still unload here — Vessara would not come if they did not). Tradition 2 (the election still happens; the old houses are not dead, only thinner).</div>
<div class="kod-step-best"><strong>Best practice</strong>Fortunes are community weather for every scene — PCs and NPCs. Derive them from material facts, not from a wish to sound epic. If the board looks comfortable after conquest and winter, re-check the strain list.</div>
</div>
