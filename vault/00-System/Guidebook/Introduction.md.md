## **Welcome to Kodranni!**

This Guide serves to provide all the necessary insight one could ask for about this **online tabletop role-playing game** system. 

There are plenty of **concepts** that are explained in detail, with context, examples. The Guide tries to follow an iterative process, though many concepts are interconnected. In these cases, please exercise patience, the Guide does its best to cover all your questions.

**Playing the game** requires the reader to understand these concepts, its design is purposefully light to avoid immersion breaking mechanic overload.

For prospective **Storytellers**, the Guide outlines the system’s principles and approaches, and offers advice on avoiding common pitfalls.

----------

### **General Applicability**

The system supports campaigns with:

 1. No magic or supernatural beings
 2. Pre-Industrial setting
 3. The characters are regular human beings

Whether it's a semi-historical campaign or a fantasy one, if the three conditions apply, the system can be of use.

----------

### **Core Ideas**
   > _"Whatever you do will be insignificant, but it is very important that you do it."_ — _Mahatma Gandhi_

-  **Immersion**: The game is designed to portray immersive reality, mechanics are meant to be non-intrusive, hopefully intuitive to understand. ***Role-play takes precedence over mechanics***.
-  **The Story of Life**: The characters in Kodranni are mortal human beings. No supernatural elements - magic, supernatural strength, etc. - is supported, nor encouraged. The character's life forms the core of the story that you tell. Their motivations, struggles, hopes and dreams create the basis of your campaign. **Death** - whether of old age or in battle - **will come**. 

> _"All we have to decide is what to do with the time that is given us."_ - _Gandalf the Grey_
-  **Player Agency**: The system is designed to be permissive, with player creativity, ideas, desperate tries, all encouraged. The system will not stand in your way, but **how you handle the risk is your responsibility!**
-  **Cooperative Struggle**: In Kodranni, your campaign is centred around a community - a Tribe, Village, Noble House - which you must nurture and maintain. Your actions - honourable or otherwise - affect the direction and future of your shared community. **If your Tribe is destroyed, your campaign ends!**
-  **Death and Continuity**: The Player's actions *can* survive their actor. A *Heroic Deed*, *Wise Counsel,* a *Miraculous Event* - may all define your Community, and more - **Echoes** of your actions can strengthen and guide future characters. **Legacy** and **Foundation Myths** provides Community-wide effect of your strongest Echoes.
-  **Automation Support**: Many of the concepts and ease of use is due to heavy automation in many regards - **the lack of experience points**, the simpler handling of a rather complicated **roll system** – is facilitated by automation. The Guide also provides a detailed description of how to set up and interact with the Automation.

### **Grim Reality vs Fun**

This is a key point, without which your campaigns will not work with this system. Kodranni is ***not*** a Horror TTRPG, nor is it supposed to shield players from consequences, or provide *any* type of plot armour to them. Kodranni is designed to mimic the **Grim Reality** of pre-industrial societies.

The fun aspect of it's campaign is in the challenge and achievement. The characters are going up against odds that are **stacked against them**, yet every step, the concepts of this system provide them with rewards that **stay within the campaign** regardless of what happens to their characters. 

Sure, in the end, all of us want to be the **Hero**, the **Savior**, or simply put, the main protagonist of the story, and Kodranni does not bar you from trying. It simply ensures that **when you fall**, you’ll be able to **continue** without losing a part of your soul and retain reminders of your prior **accomplishments** built into your campaign.

Two behavioural patterns ruin this experience:

 1. Players who **churn out characters designed to die**, for the fun and chaos of it - the typical question of:
_*Why care for a character destined to die?*_
>	 Yup, it's fun short term, but the player will get bored of it, the campaign will get ruined, immersion will be gone, and then it becomes the most boring campaign ruining experience they had ever felt.
2. Storytellers who **punish for the sake of punishment**:
_*Why would I make a character that the Storyteller will just kill?*_
> You really don't need to overplay the grim nature, the system does it for you, most of the time you'll be as relieved as your players when a risky plan succeeds. And if you're an ass, your players will be playing against you, not their intended foes in-game.

In short, **Embrace the Contrast**. Don't shield, allow your players the freedom for creativity, craft stories that are grim, but filled with meaning.

## **Dice Mechanics**

Similar to other TTRPG's, action resolution is handled by throwing dice. Whenever a **player** or **storyteller** needs to resolve an action, they discuss and throw appropriate dice to decide how that action turns out. Very standard -- *so far*. 

----------

### Dice Types and Pools

In Kodranni, there are **three types** of dice used:

 - **6**-sided dice - further referred to as **d6**.
 - **8**-sided dice - further referred to as **d8**.
 - **12**-sided dice - further referred to as **d12**.

Rolls consist of only one of these three types – creating d6, d8, or d12 dice pools.

----------

### Marks of Success

Any die that lands on 5 or higher is considered a **Mark of Success**, or simply a **Mark**.

The **different dice types** thus have varying probabilities of resulting in a **Mark**:
 - **6**-sided dice (**d6**): 33%
 - **8**-sided dice (**d8**): 50%
 - **12**-sided dice (**d12**): 66%

Unlike similar dice pool systems, where a pool of dice is thrown to determine the outcome, Kodranni’s **Mark** system is slightly different. Mostly interpretational difference:

> These ***'Marks of Success'***, rather than just a binary pass/fail result, interpret rolls with layers of outcomes, assessing the **effectiveness**, **quality**, or **progress** toward achieving a goal.

What's the difference? The players are **not** trying to achieve an **arbitrary threshold**, they try their best not knowing what outcomes they might face. The burden is on the **Storyteller** to **interpret the results** of a roll, increasing the rewards and penalties based on the Marks rolled.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

This is where ***context*** comes in. In any situation, there are certain factors that influence the outcome and the odds of success of an action. These factors are often **innumerable**, weighing the odds on both the side of success and failure. However, in most cases, it's ***fairly easy*** to assess which side has the advantage.

Different dice types simulate Advantage or Disadvantage, incorporating **context** into any situation.

- **Advantage**: In favourable situations (such as beneficial terrain, superior knowledge, or support from others), the dice tier of the roll **upgrades**. 
> For example, a **trained hunter** (who would normally roll **d8** dice pools) could roll **d12** when tracking a wounded prey with his hounds.
  
- **Disadvantage**: In challenging circumstances (e.g., poor weather, hostile terrain, or in a disadvantageous fight), the dice tier of the roll **downgrades**.
> For example, a **veteran soldier** ( who would otherwise roll **d8** dice pools) might roll **d6** when surrounded by foes on multiple sides.
