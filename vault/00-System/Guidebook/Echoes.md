## **Echoes**

An **Echo** is a deed, decision, or event that a character chooses to carry. It is the concrete mark of what the character cares about — personal, shared, or community-defining.

The **player** elevates a moment into an Echo. The Storyteller may veto only for tone or outright nonsense. One or two Echoes may be declared at character creation; new ones may be created at any time thereafter when a deed warrants it.

### Weight and Capacity

Every Echo has a weight:

- **Individual** (1) — known and cared for by the character alone.
- **Group** (2) — shared among a small circle of the involved, not yet enough to shift a Fortune by itself.
- **Pivotal** (3) — directly tied to the state of the community and capable of moving a Fortune.

A character’s **Echo capacity** equals their maximum Exertion pool (Resolve + Constitution + Charisma).

- While the total weight of carried Echoes exceeds capacity, every roll that involves any of the character’s Echoes suffers **–1 die** to the pool.
- While a character carries **no Echoes at all**, every roll suffers **–1 die**. This is **Decadence** — the cost of caring about nothing.

> The load is not abstract. Too many burdens crack a person. None at all leaves them hollow.

### Invocation

When a roll’s context matches one of the character’s Echoes, the player may spend **one extra Exertion die** beyond the normal limit of one.

### Resolution

An Echo resolves when the table agrees the original concern is settled, broken, transcended, or made irrelevant. Resolution produces one of the following:

- a personal **Trait**,
- a **relationship**, a favour or shift,
- a narrative event/fact,
- or, if the Echo was **Pivotal**, a shift to one **Fortune** and inclusion among the **Foundation Myths**.

Personal (weight 1) Echoes usually die with the character unless the table elevates them. Group and Pivotal Echoes persist and may be claimed by successors.

## **Fortunes**

The community is tracked by five soft measures called **Fortunes**. They never produce absolute numbers (no head-counts of cattle, no exact population tallies). They exist only as contextual levers.

| Fortune     | What it reflects                          |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Vitality**   | People, health, capacity to absorb loss   |
| **Cohesion**   | Trust, internal order, willingness to act together |
| **Surplus**    | Material buffer — food, tools, stores     |
| **Standing**   | External reputation, treaties, fear or respect |
| **Tradition**  | Shared memory, lore, what the community believes about itself |

Each Fortune is rated **0–3**:

- **0** Crisis  
- **1** Strained  
- **2** Steady  
- **3** Abundance

## **Foundation Myths**

The three most recent **resolved Pivotal Echoes** become the community’s active **Foundation Myths**. Older ones fade from the active set as new pivotal events take their place.

A Foundation Myth exerts a substantial, narrow effect on a specific set of actions. Possible effects include:

- free extra Exertion spend, or forced extra Exertion cost, on matching rolls  
- situational Advantage or Disadvantage  
- one or more specific faces locked onto the Omen die for community-relevant rolls  
- extra or reduced Practice gain on matching Skills
- influence on the starting point and shift thresholds of a Tide (via advantage/disadvantage, and concrete shift amounts)
- a temporary or permanent Trait available (or denied) to all members of the community  

Myths are living. They can be reinforced, corrupted, or overwritten by later events. They are the concrete way a character’s greatest deeds outlive the character and reshape the community that remains.
