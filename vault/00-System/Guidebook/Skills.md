### Skills and Archetypes

Skills are practiced abilities. Unlike Foundations they improve through actual use — both success and failure teach, though failure teaches more.

Skills are organised under seven **Archetypes** (personified categories). These are not character classes. They are simply the broad domains into which individual Skills fall:

- Warrior  
- Traveller  
- Artisan  
- Alchemist  
- Chronicler  
- Elder  
- Thief  

Each individual Skill under an Archetype is rated 0–3. A rating of 0 means the character has no meaningful practice in that specific Skill. The Storyteller selects the single most fitting Skill (and therefore its Archetype) from the player’s description of what they are attempting. The player may argue for a different pair, but the final decision rests with the Storyteller.

> The list of concrete Skills belonging to each Archetype is large and lives in its own chapter. This section only explains the concept and the improvement rules.

**Improvement**

Every time a Skill is used in a roll it may generate Practice (tracked automatically by the bot).

- **Opposed rolls**: Practice = the Marks difference. If the character lost the contest, add +2.  
- **Unopposed rolls**: +2 Practice if the roll contained at least one failed die, plus +1 Practice for every two Marks of Success (rounded down).

Practice accumulates against thresholds:

| Current Skill | Base Threshold |
|---------------|----------------|
| 0 → 1         | 24             |
| 1 → 2         | 48             |
| 2 → 3         | 72             |

These thresholds are modified by the Skill’s ruling Foundation (the single Foundation permanently associated with that Skill):

- Foundation 3 (above average) → thresholds halved  
- Foundation 1 (below average) → thresholds doubled  
- Foundation 2 → base values  

High inborn potential therefore accelerates learning. Low potential slows it. The bot handles all accumulation and level-ups. Players never see raw Practice numbers unless they ask.

> Struggle teaches. Clean excellence still teaches, but more slowly. That is intentional.
