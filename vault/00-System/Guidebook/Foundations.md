## **Foundations, Skills, Traits and Exertion**

These four elements form the living skeleton of a character. Foundations are the bone — inborn and nearly unchanging. Skills are the muscle grown through use. Traits are the scars, quirks and callings that make one person distinct from another. Exertion is the breath and the will that lets a character push past ordinary limits.

Together they determine the size and character of every dice pool. The rules that follow assume the dice system described in DiceMechanics is already understood.

----------

### Foundations

Foundations represent a character’s inborn potential. They are not trained abilities and they do not improve through ordinary play. Aging, catastrophic injury or rare transformative events may shift them, but such changes are exceptional and always narrative.

There are nine Foundations, grouped by domain:

**Physical**  
- **Strength** — raw power, lifting, striking force, physical dominance  
- **Dexterity** — speed, precision, balance, fine motor control  
- **Constitution** — endurance, hardiness, resistance to fatigue and hardship  

**Mental**  
- **Intellect** — reasoning, learning, memory, problem-solving (pure recall is folded here)  
- **Perception** — awareness, observation, noticing the hidden or the out-of-place  
- **Resolve** — willpower, mental fortitude, the capacity to keep going when the mind wants to quit  

**Social**  
- **Charisma** — presence, force of personality, the ability to draw others  
- **Guile** — cunning, misdirection, social sleight-of-hand  
- **Authority** — natural command, the weight that makes others listen or yield  

Each Foundation is rated on a narrow scale:

- **1** — below average  
- **2** — average  
- **3** — above average  

Values of 0 or 4+ exist only for extreme cases (severe disability or legendary freaks of nature) and should be rare.

When a roll is called for, the Storyteller names the single most relevant Foundation based on the player’s description of intent. Primitive actions (running, hauling, pure endurance, basic observation, composure under pressure, etc.) use Foundation alone.

> Foundations are the ceiling of what a character can readily become. A high Foundation does not guarantee mastery, but it makes mastery easier to reach.
