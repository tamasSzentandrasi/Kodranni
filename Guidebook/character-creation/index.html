Campaign & Character Creation

Character Creation

“The old forefathers lived in their posterity, filled them out with their will, and wrought their achievements through them anew.”
— Vilhelm Grønbech

Prerequisite: the Storyteller has finished Campaign Setup. The campaign frame, community, and system constraints already exist.

Character creation has two phases:

1. Private prep

You and the Storyteller only. Concept, Foundations, Skills. Numbers stay secret until The Weighing.

2. The Weighing

The whole table. Four steps in order: Birth Omen → Guiding Hand → Who do we see? → The Wanting.

Private prep

Section titled “Private prep”

The character record is opened as a draft (automation when available): Foundation and Skill budgets are granted so spends can be checked as you go. See Automation — Weighing.

Concept

Section titled “Concept”

Write or speak a short concept. Two to five sentences. Longer is usually worse.

It must include a concrete tie to the community — blood, debt, marriage, sponsorship, shared loss, forced service, or something equally solid. A foreigner may arrive; they cannot stay an outsider. Weak or abstract ties are rejected.

Also tell the Storyteller what you want from play: kinds of story, what you fear for this person, what you will risk. That helps them work with you.

The Storyteller may reject a concept that does not fit the frame or is offered in bad faith. That call is final.

Foundations

Section titled “Foundations”

All nine Foundations start at 1.

You get 4 free points.

Step Cost
1 → 2 1
2 → 3 2

Maximum 3. A 0 or 4 needs Storyteller approval and a matching Trait.

Skills

Section titled “Skills”

You get 12 points for Skills.

Costs stack:

Step Cost
0 → 1 1
1 → 2 2
2 → 3 3

Rank 3 in one Skill therefore costs 6. Possible; expensive.

Keep all of these numbers private until The Weighing. Do not discuss them with the other players.

The Weighing

Section titled “The Weighing”

Four steps. Walk them in order. Private rolls first; then the table speaks; then you spend what you earned — or lose it.

Step What happens Who sees it
1. Birth Omen d20 → Foundation points You + Storyteller only
2. Guiding Hand d20 → Skill points You + Storyteller only
3. Who do we see? You speak; others may claim; marks and Words Whole table
4. The Wanting Spend Words on your sheet Whole table

The Weighing

Private points first. Then claims. Then Words spent on yourself — or lost.

What: Roll a d20 (Omen). Only you and the Storyteller see it.

Points: result halved and rounded up → 1–10 Foundation points. Spend them on Foundations at the normal costs (1→2 costs 1; 2→3 costs 2).

Why: private luck for the character’s inborn range. Not a public prophecy. Do not discuss the number at the table.

ExampleDie shows 11 → 6 Foundation points. Mara spends 2 to raise Resolve 1→2, 2 for Guile 1→2, 2 for Intellect 1→2. No one else hears the roll.
Best practiceSpend toward the concept you already wrote. Do not re-roll for flavour. The table will define the person by speech in step 3, not by this die.

What: Roll a second d20 for Skills. The full result (1–20) is extra Skill points. Spend at normal cumulative costs (0→1 = 1, 1→2 = 2, 2→3 = 3).

Still private until step 3.

ExampleDie shows 9. Mara puts 6 into Negotiation 0→3 (1+2+3) and 3 into Tradecraft 0→2 (1+2).
Best practiceFuel the same concept. Guiding Hand is not a second draft of who the character is.

This is the public half of creation. One character at a time.

1. Self-claim. The Storyteller asks: “Who do we see?” The player answers in two to four spoken sentences. No monologue.

2. Others claim. Each other player may offer one short claim: “I see ___.” — an adjective or phrase not already in the written concept or the self-claim.

3. Accept or refuse.

  • A seconder is required for any claim to stand.
  • The target and the Storyteller may jointly refuse.
  • Only one accepted claim per speaker per target. After one lands, that speaker is done with that person.

4. When a claim is accepted, two things happen at once:

  1. Speaker gains one Word — spent later in The Wanting on their own character, not on the target.
  2. Storyteller applies a mark on the target now: Skill points in a fitting Archetype, a small Foundation shift, a Trait, starting Harm, or another consequence that sharpens who they are. The goal is definition, not punishment. The speaker does not choose the mark.

The Storyteller ends when a full pass of the circle produces nothing further worth accepting.

Speaker gets

One Word per accepted claim — for their own Wanting only.

Target gets

A Storyteller mark immediately. Not a free gift chosen by the speaker.

ExampleMara has said who she is. Cal: “I see a hard bargainer.” Someone seconds. Mara and the Storyteller accept. Cal banks 1 Word. The Storyteller marks Mara now (+2 Negotiation, or Trait “Hard bargainer”). Cal does not spend on Mara’s sheet.
Best practiceClaim things that fit what you already heard in concept and prep — not jokes. Accepted claims stick for the campaign.

Words come only from claims you made about other people in step 3. You spend them here on your own character — or leave them unspent and lose them.

The person you spoke about was already marked when the claim was accepted. The Wanting does not touch their sheet. It reshapes yours.

Each Word buys one menu option. You name the Foundations or Skills on your sheet. The Storyteller approves or denies. If a Negative Trait is required, the Storyteller chooses it.

+1 to one named Foundation

Pay: remove 3 skill points of your choice

+2 Foundation points (split)

Must go to two different Foundations. Pay: remove 5 skill points or take one Negative Trait (Storyteller chooses)

+5 skill points

Pay: −1 from one named Foundation of your choice

One Positive named Trait

Pay: remove 3 skill points of your choice

That is the full menu. Nothing else.

ExampleCal has one Word. He takes “+5 skill points,” pays −1 Constitution on his own sheet, and names Skills the Storyteller confirms. Mara’s sheet does not change in this spend — she was already marked when the claim landed.
Best practiceSpend every Word. Unspent Words are gone. Do not invent purchases that are not on the menu.

If you skip claims or troll the circle, you earn no Words. Others will spend what they earned. Participate or fall behind on your own sheet.

After the Weighing

Section titled “After the Weighing”

Lock these before the character enters the campaign record:

Item Rule
Echoes From the concept and accepted claims. Community tie required. Capacity = max(Strength, Dexterity) + Intellect + Authority. Zero Echoes = Decadence.
Hierarchy Position if any; default Outcast. Standing is claimed in play, not invented as a full ladder at creation.
Traits Locked.
Inventory Only named items that already fit the concept. No free packs. The rest is found or bought in play.

Then write the character into the record (automation when available).
